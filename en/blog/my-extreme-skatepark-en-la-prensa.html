A month of My Extreme Skatepark travelling the world

Published on 9 November 2026

I co-designed a board game with Naotaka Shimamoto, a designer in Japan I still have not met in person. In October it went out into the world, and what followed kept arriving in languages I do not speak.

My Extreme Skatepark is a box that turns into a cardboard skatepark: you flick a tiny board with your finger and try to land it without hitting the obstacles. It is published by itten as part of their Funbrick series.

Japan heard about it first

On 16 October, Table Games in the World — the site where Japanese players find out about everything — reported that itten was releasing three new titles in mid-November. One of them was ours, credited as “D.A.ピノ＆島本直尚”. Seeing your own surname written in katakana for the first time is a strange and very good feeling.

Then came the Essen preview

Before anyone had played it, the game was already travelling on its own. Meeple Mountain put it on their list of the 21 most anticipated games of SPIEL Essen 2026. Backseat Gamer gave it a piece of its own from the UK. In Spain, 2d6mag covered the announcement and came back to it in their Essen special. Even a Slovak newsletter, Doberacia Hromádka, named it among the week's new releases.

That is what happens in the weeks before a fair: a game exists only as a few photos and two lines of description, and people still decide whether it interests them.

The fair, and the other fair

It premiered in two places at almost the same time: SPIEL Essen in Germany, and the autumn Game Market in Tokyo. sumikai wrote about Essen after talking with the itten team about how it had gone. Table Games in the World reported from the Game Market.

And then someone actually played it

Up to that point it was all text. What changes things is watching someone play.

The The Dice Tower one surprised me most: it is a play stream with thousands of people watching, and at some point our little box shows up. non solo Pane tried it at the fair itself, and Miss Merc007 gave it a chapter of its own in an unboxing.

What the people who played it say

On BoardGameGeek the game averages 5.85 from 26 ratings and 7 comments. Opinions are split, and that seems healthy to me: it is a dexterity game, and dexterity games divide people.

A player in Belgium bought it at Essen and wrote that it was unlike anything he had played in a long time. Another, from Salt Lake City, gave it a 2 because he felt the skateboard would not obey him. Both can be true at once, and both are useful to me.

What comes next

In mid-November it reaches Japanese shops. It is the first game of mine published outside Chile, so I am seeing all of this for the first time.
