Designer Diary: My Extreme Skatepark

Or how returning to skateboarding brought a South American game to Japan

by Daniel Arosteguy (Danny Blue Jet)

Back in 2022, the Chilean publisher Fractal Games announced their annual game design contest, Llévalo a la Mesa ("Take It to the Table"). The prize was straightforward but huge for any designer: getting your game published. Every year, the contest comes with a specific constraint or theme, and that year's challenge was clear: design a game using only cards.

Around that same time, life outside of board games took an unexpected turn. When I was a kid, I used to skateboard, but I gave it up after a nasty ankle sprain during a fall — that was enough to put the board away for years. Fast forward to 2022: a really nice new skatepark had opened here in Osorno, and I started going regularly with my son.

Watching him skate — and feeling the energy of the park — reignited something in me. After all those years, I decided to step back onto a board. As I spent hours at the park watching lines, tricks, failed attempts, and the flow of movement, my brain naturally started translating those elements into mechanics. I realized the rhythm of skating, the risk vs. reward of landing tricks, and the progression of learning a line could fit perfectly into a card system.

So I asked myself: What is the absolute best part of skateboarding?

To me, the answer was obvious: landing a trick and literally flying on your board. That sensation of momentum, timing, and triumph was the exact core experience I wanted to translate to the tabletop.

The main challenge became figuring out how to recreate that thrill using nothing but cards. How do you make a player feel the anticipation, the precision, and the instant satisfaction of nailing a trick — or the tension of pushing your luck — with just a hand of cards?

My very first prototype leaned into dexterity to capture that physical feel. It included three specific tricks, each translated into a physical action with the cards:

The Pop / Flip: You placed your card right at the edge of the table and flicked it upward with your finger, aiming to make it fly through the air and land face up.

The Shove-it: Just like the real skateboarding trick where the board rotates 180 degrees beneath your feet, you had to spin the card mid-air so it landed flipped.

The Grind: Two cards leaned against each other to form a rail, and you had to carefully balance your board-card right on top of it, just like building a house of cards.

Just as I finished the prototype, near the end of the year, unexpected news hit me: I was laid off from my job as a music teacher. It was a tough moment, but having spent seven years with the institution, I received a severance payout.

Instead of seeing it purely as a setback, I decided to reframe it as an opportunity. I looked at the payout, thought about the game I had just built, and realized: This is my moment to self-publish my game.

During this self-publishing phase, my good friend and artist Imnoveja jumped on board to create custom artwork for the game box and components. He designed vibrant, urban art that really brought the skateboarding culture to life, preparing the project so we could print and sell it locally in Chile.

Since it was summertime, I took the prototype to my friends at Ludoismo in the board gaming community in Valdivia to show them what I had built. They played it and absolutely loved it.

They pointed out that the physical dexterity mechanism felt genuinely innovative. That feedback really caught my attention. They had attended major international conventions like SPIEL in Essen, and we talked about how rare it is to stumble upon truly fresh mechanics in an industry where many designs start to feel repetitive. They were constantly on the lookout for new ideas, and they felt this game had a unique spark.

To top it off, and to lend me a hand financially after my layoff, they made me an incredible offer: they offered to take charge of publishing the game and handling its license distribution.

In my mind, that was it — I felt like I had already "won" the contest I originally set out to enter. With their backing, we jumped straight into development.

One of the first big ideas brought to the table was replacing standard playing cards with custom cardboard skateboards. I loved the idea, so I started making small cardboard deck prototypes and testing them out, even taking them into my music classes to playtest with my students.

That turned out to be a wonderful surprise. The kids immediately resonated with the game, and they even started drawing and painting their own customized graphic designs right onto the cardboard decks. Seeing their creativity and enthusiasm made it clear that we were onto something truly special.

My friends at Ludoismo started preparing everything to take the prototype to SPIEL in Essen. When they returned, they brought back the most amazing news: they had met with the team at ITTEN and signed a contract with them to officially publish the game globally!

As time passed, the team at ITTEN, together with co-designer Naotaka Shimamoto, kept sending updates on how the game was evolving under their care. While it went through significant changes during development, I was always thrilled to watch the process. They bring so much experience to the table, and even though I hadn't played their earlier titles before, I've always admired their catalog for its clever, highly innovative design philosophy. Seeing how they approached refining and polishing my concept was an absolute joy.

Now, I am beyond thrilled that the game has finally made its debut this year at Game Market in Tokyo and at SPIEL in Essen!

Seeing this project journey all the way from a personal spark at a local skatepark in Chile to two of the biggest tabletop events in the world is a dream come true. I truly hope players around the globe enjoy flipping, grinding, and pulling off tricks at the table as much as I enjoyed creating it. And of course, my biggest hope for the future is to one day walk into a local board game shop right here in Chile and see it sitting proudly on the shelves.

Thank you for reading, and happy gaming!
